# Unslop

Make the requested text sound like a person explaining something clearly. Preserve facts, uncertainty, attribution, and the user's intended voice. This is editing guidance, not a reason to invent opinions or change behavior in code.

## Edit

1. Identify the audience, purpose, and main point. Lead with what the reader needs to know or do.
2. Replace vague praise and abstract claims with facts, concrete actions, or measured results. Remove unsupported attributions such as "experts believe"; name the source when one exists.
3. Cut filler, repeated summaries, excessive hedging, canned chatbot phrases, and flattery. Keep qualifiers that communicate real uncertainty.
4. Prefer plain words and active verbs. Replace words such as "utilize", "leverage", and "facilitate" with "use" or "help" when they mean the same thing. Explain necessary technical terms rather than erasing precision.
5. Split dense sentences and connect each paragraph around one idea. Vary sentence length naturally. Avoid forced groups of three, synonym cycling, and comparisons that introduce an alternative the reader never asked about.
6. Use punctuation and formatting for clarity. Avoid repeated dash clauses, mid-sentence colon crutches, decorative emojis, excessive bold, and headings that restate the next sentence. Use lists for parallel items or steps, not as a substitute for explanation.
7. Add voice only where the source supports it. A specific observation or reasoned judgment is useful; fabricated feelings and performative informality are not.
8. Read the result once more. Remove lines that could fit unchanged in any project's documentation, and check that simplification has preserved the original meaning.

For documentation, comments, and user-facing strings in code, edit the language within the requested scope and preserve behavior. Use the workspace's normal file-editing tools when files are requested; use a direct reply for pasted text. Keep meaningful technical names and links. Report material factual gaps instead of filling them with plausible text.
